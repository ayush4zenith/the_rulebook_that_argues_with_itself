# SGSITS Indore — Undergraduate Academic Ordinance
## Ordinance No. 4 | B.Tech / B.E. Programmes | 2025–2026
### Shri G. S. Institute of Technology and Science, Indore
#### (Autonomous Institution | Affiliated to RGPV Bhopal | Approved by AICTE & UGC)

---

> **PREAMBLE**: This Ordinance governs all academic matters pertaining to the four-year B.Tech / B.E. programmes offered by Shri G. S. Institute of Technology and Science (SGSITS), Indore. It is issued under the authority of the Academic Council and is binding on all enrolled students, faculty, and administrative personnel. Where a conflict arises between this Ordinance and an earlier circular or departmental notice, this Ordinance shall prevail unless a subsequent Academic Council resolution explicitly overrides it.

---

## Chapter 1 — Definitions

**1.1** "Institute" refers to Shri G. S. Institute of Technology and Science, Indore.

**1.2** "Academic Council" refers to the supreme academic body of the Institute constituted under the Institute's statutes.

**1.3** "Board of Studies" (BoS) refers to the subject-specific body responsible for curriculum design and assessment schemes for each department.

**1.4** "Controller of Examinations" (CoE) refers to the officer appointed by the Director to manage all matters related to examinations, result processing, and certification.

**1.5** "CGPA" (Cumulative Grade Point Average) is the weighted average of grade points earned across all semesters, calculated by dividing the total credit-weighted grade points by total credits attempted.

**1.6** "SGPA" (Semester Grade Point Average) is the weighted average of grade points for a single semester.

**1.7** "Grade Point" is the numerical equivalent of a letter grade as per the 10-point scale adopted by the Institute in conformity with UGC guidelines.

**1.8** "Credit" refers to the unit of academic weightage assigned to each course component (theory, tutorial, practical).

**1.9** "End-Semester Examination" (ESE) refers to the formal written/practical examination conducted at the conclusion of each semester.

**1.10** "Continuous Evaluation" (CE) refers to the internal assessment components conducted throughout the semester, including mid-semester tests, assignments, quizzes, and practicals.

**1.11** "Backlog" refers to a subject in which a student has failed to secure the minimum passing grade and must re-appear in subsequent examinations.

**1.12** "Detention" refers to the status in which a student is not permitted to proceed to the next semester or year due to failure to meet minimum academic or attendance requirements.

**1.13** "UFM" refers to the use of Unfair Means in examinations, including but not limited to copying, impersonation, and possession of prohibited materials.

**1.14** "HOD" refers to the Head of Department of the relevant academic department.

**1.15** "Chief Warden" refers to the faculty member appointed by the Director as the overall in-charge of the Institute's hostel administration.

## Chapter 2 — Admission and Registration

### 2.1 Eligibility for Admission

2.1.1 Admission to the first year of the B.Tech programme shall be as per the prevailing admission policy of the Government of Madhya Pradesh, through the DTE MP counselling process or the Institute's own spot admission round as applicable.

2.1.2 Candidates must have passed 10+2 (Higher Secondary Certificate) or equivalent examination with Physics and Mathematics as compulsory subjects, and Chemistry/Computer Science/Biology/Technical Vocational subject as an optional subject, with a minimum aggregate of 45% marks (40% for SC/ST candidates as per MP government norms).

2.1.3 Admission through JEE (Main) is considered for the NRI/management quota seats as defined by AICTE and DTE MP.

### 2.2 Re-Registration / Enrolment Each Semester

2.2.1 Every student must register (enrol) for the ensuing semester by paying the prescribed semester fees within the deadline notified by the Academic Section. Registration is mandatory for attendance to be counted.

2.2.2 Students who fail to register within the prescribed deadline will have their attendance marked from the first day of teaching, leading to a reduced denominator total that can disadvantage their attendance percentage. No retrospective attendance credit is given for the period before late registration.

2.2.3 Students with outstanding dues to the Institute — including but not limited to tuition fees, hostel fees, library fines, or laboratory breakage charges — shall not be allowed to register for the next semester until all dues are cleared.

### 2.3 Branch Change

2.3.1 Branch change at the end of the first year (after Semester I and II) is permitted, subject to the following conditions:

(a) The student must have cleared all subjects of Semester I and II without any backlog at the first attempt;
(b) The student's CGPA at the end of Semester II must be sufficient to meet the cut-off for the desired branch, which is determined annually by the Dean Academics based on available seats;
(c) Seats available for branch change shall not exceed 10% of the sanctioned intake of the target branch;
(d) Reservation norms as per the Government of Madhya Pradesh shall be maintained within the branch-change seats.

2.3.2 Branch change applications must be submitted to the Academic Section within 15 days of the declaration of Semester II results. Late applications will not be entertained.

2.3.3 Students who have availed SGSITS's institutional scholarship tied to their original branch are advised to consult the Scholarship Cell before applying for branch change, as scholarship terms may vary by programme.

### 2.4 Fee Adjustment on Branch Change / Transfer

**2.4.1** Tuition fees paid for institute transfer or internal branch sliding after Round 2 of DTE counselling are strictly non-refundable and non-adjustable against subsequent semesters. Students seeking transfer to another institution or changing branches through DTE Round 3 or later do so at their own financial risk, and no claim for fee refund will be entertained by the Institute.

**2.4.2** In the event of an upgraded branch allotment under institutional sliding (i.e., where a student is internally upgraded to a higher-preference branch within SGSITS itself, based on CGPA merit), surplus tuition and development fees deposited during the initial round shall be adjusted against the third-semester fee dues on a pro-rata basis. The Finance Officer shall calculate and communicate the adjustment amount to the student within 30 days of the branch change confirmation.

> **[CONTRADICTION POINT B — see contradictions.md]**: Clause 2.4.1 states fees after Round 2 are "strictly non-refundable and non-adjustable against subsequent semesters." Clause 2.4.2 mandates fee adjustment for internal branch upgrades. These two clauses apply to overlapping scenarios — an internal upgrade under institutional sliding can occur after Round 2 — and they directly contradict each other on whether fees can be adjusted.

## Chapter 3 — Curriculum Structure and Credits

### 3.1 Programme Duration and Structure

3.1.1 The B.Tech programme is of four-year (eight-semester) duration. Each semester is of approximately 18–20 weeks, inclusive of teaching weeks and end-semester examination weeks.

3.1.2 The curriculum for each programme is as approved by the Board of Studies and ratified by the Academic Council. The curriculum shall contain the following components:

| Component | Typical Credits per Semester |
|---|---|
| Theory Courses | 12–18 credits |
| Laboratory Practicals | 2–6 credits |
| Tutorial Sessions | 1–3 credits |
| Seminars / Mini Projects | 0–2 credits |
| Major Project (Semester VIII) | 8–12 credits |

3.1.3 The total credit requirement for the award of the B.Tech degree is a minimum of **170 credits**, with programme-specific requirements as specified in the Scheme of Teaching and Examination for each branch.

### 3.2 Credit System

3.2.1 Credits are assigned to each course component as follows:
- 1 credit = 1 lecture hour per week for a 16-week teaching semester
- 1 credit = 2 tutorial hours per week
- 1 credit = 2 practical hours per week

3.2.2 Credits are earned only upon successful completion of the course (i.e., the student secures the minimum passing grade). Credits for failed courses are not earned and must be re-attempted.

## Chapter 4 — Assessment and Grading

### 4.1 Assessment Structure

4.1.1 Assessment for each theory course shall be divided as follows:

| Component | Weightage |
|---|---|
| Continuous Evaluation (CE) | 30% of total marks |
| End-Semester Examination (ESE) | 70% of total marks |

4.1.2 Continuous Evaluation (CE) marks shall be awarded based on:
- Mid-Semester Test 1 (MST-1): conducted around Week 6–7
- Mid-Semester Test 2 (MST-2): conducted around Week 14–15
- Assignments, quizzes, and class participation
- Attendance component (as prescribed by the Board of Studies, up to 5 marks)

4.1.3 For practical/laboratory courses, the assessment shall be:
- Internal Practical Record (Viva + File): 60% of practical marks
- End-Semester Practical Examination: 40% of practical marks

4.1.4 No student shall be awarded CE marks for a session they did not attend unless an authorised absence has been granted under Chapter 5.

### 4.2 Passing Criteria

4.2.1 To pass a theory course, a student must:
(a) Score a minimum of **35% in the End-Semester Examination** separately; AND
(b) Score a minimum of **40% in the aggregate** (CE + ESE combined).

4.2.2 To pass a practical course, a student must:
(a) Score a minimum of **50% in aggregate** of internal practical and end-semester practical components.

4.2.3 A student who secures less than 35% in the ESE alone — even if their aggregate is 40% or above — shall be considered to have failed the theory course and must appear for the subject in the backlog/supplementary examination.

### 4.3 Grading Scale

The following 10-point grading scale is adopted by SGSITS in conformity with UGC guidelines:

| Marks Range (%) | Letter Grade | Grade Point |
|---|---|---|
| 90 – 100 | O (Outstanding) | 10 |
| 75 – 89 | A+ (Excellent) | 9 |
| 65 – 74 | A (Very Good) | 8 |
| 55 – 64 | B+ (Good) | 7 |
| 50 – 54 | B (Average) | 6 |
| 45 – 49 | C (Satisfactory) | 5 |
| 40 – 44 | D (Pass) | 4 |
| Below 40 | F (Fail) | 0 |
| Absent | Ab | 0 |

4.3.2 A grade of "F" carries zero grade points and the credit is NOT awarded. The course must be repeated.

4.3.3 A grade of "Ab" (Absent from ESE without prior approval) is treated as "F" for all academic purposes.

### 4.4 SGPA Calculation

4.4.1 SGPA for a semester is calculated as:

**SGPA = Σ(Ci × GPi) / ΣCi**

Where Ci is the credit assigned to the i-th course and GPi is the grade point earned in that course.

4.4.2 Failed courses (Grade F or Ab) contribute 0 grade points but their credits remain in the denominator, thus reducing the SGPA.

### 4.5 CGPA Calculation

4.5.1 CGPA at any point is calculated as:

**CGPA = Σ(Ci × GPi) [all attempted semesters] / Σ(Ci) [all attempted semesters]**

4.5.2 Upon successful re-appearance and passing a backlog course, the new grade replaces the F grade in the CGPA calculation. The F/Ab grade is expunged from the CGPA denominator's grade point contribution (but not from the transcript, which retains the academic history).

### 4.6 Re-evaluation / Rechecking of Answer Scripts

4.6.1 A student who is dissatisfied with their marks in the End-Semester Examination may apply for re-evaluation (re-checking) of their answer script by submitting an application to the Controller of Examinations within **15 days** of the declaration of results, along with a fee of **₹500 per subject**.

4.6.2 The re-evaluation shall be carried out by an examiner different from the original evaluator.

4.6.3 If the re-evaluated marks differ from the original marks by **more than 10%** of the total marks of the paper, the student is entitled to a full refund of the re-evaluation fee.

4.6.4 The re-evaluated mark shall be the final mark. No further appeal for re-evaluation of the same paper shall be entertained.

4.6.5 If the re-evaluation results in a higher mark that changes the student's grade, the new grade shall be incorporated. If the re-evaluation results in a **lower mark** than originally awarded, the student's original mark shall stand. The Institute shall not penalise a student by reducing their marks through the re-evaluation process.

### 4.7 Detention and Semester Progression

**4.7.1** A student shall be **detained** (not permitted to proceed to the next year/semester group) if:

(a) The student's SGPA for the current semester is **below 4.0 (i.e., below Grade D on average)**; OR
(b) The student has **more than 4 active backlogs** at any point; OR
(c) The student's attendance falls below the minimum prescribed threshold (see Chapter 5).

**4.7.2** The minimum SGPA to avoid detention is **4.0 per semester**. A student achieving exactly 4.0 SGPA is eligible to proceed.

4.7.3 A detained student must repeat the failed semester(s). Repetition is subject to paying the full semester fees again and attending all sessions as a regular student.

4.7.4 A student who is detained for a second time in the same year/semester shall be referred to the Academic Council for a review, which may recommend discontinuation from the programme.

## Chapter 5 — Attendance Requirements

### 5.1 Mandatory Attendance

**5.1.1** Attendance at all scheduled sessions — lectures, tutorials, and laboratory practicals — is compulsory for every enrolled student.

### 5.2 Minimum Attendance Threshold for Examinations

**Clause 4.10.1** *(renumbered here as §5.2.1 for cross-referencing convenience — the original clause number is retained for traceability)*

**5.2.1 / Clause 4.10.1**: A candidate shall only be permitted to appear in the End-Semester Examination if they have attended at least **75% of classes** held in theory and practicals **separately**. The 75% threshold applies independently to theory sessions and practical sessions for each subject. A student may meet the 75% requirement for theory but be barred from the practical examination if practical attendance is below 75%, and vice versa.

5.2.2 Attendance is calculated as:

**Attendance % = (Sessions Attended / Total Sessions Held) × 100**

5.2.3 Attendance records are maintained by subject teachers and uploaded to the Institute's academic portal within 48 hours of each session. Students are advised to monitor their attendance on the portal regularly.

5.2.4 If a discrepancy is found in the recorded attendance, the student must bring it to the attention of the subject teacher within 5 working days. Disputes older than 5 working days shall not be entertained unless accompanied by documentary evidence (e.g., signed class sheet copy).

### 5.3 Consequences of Falling Below 75% Threshold

5.3.1 Students whose attendance in any subject falls below 75% will be listed on the **Shortage of Attendance** (SoA) register maintained by the Examination Section.

5.3.2 The SoA list shall be displayed on the Institute notice board and the academic portal at the following milestones:
- After Week 8 (mid-semester warning)
- After Week 14 (pre-final warning)
- After the last teaching day (final bar list)

5.3.3 A student whose name appears on the final bar list for any subject shall **not be admitted to the End-Semester Examination hall** for that subject. The Hall Ticket of barred students will indicate "BARRED" against the relevant subject.

5.3.4 Invigilators are required to check the bar list before distributing question papers. A student found to have entered the examination hall for a barred subject shall have their paper invalidated.

### 5.4 Condonation of Attendance Deficiency by the Director

**Clause 4.10.4**: The Director, upon recommendation of the HOD and production of an authorised **District Hospital** medical certificate (or equivalent government medical authority certificate), may condone attendance shortages down to a floor of **60%**. This means:

- A student with attendance between **60% and 74%** may apply for condonation.
- Condonation is **not automatic** — it requires a formal written application supported by medical documentation.
- **Under no circumstances shall any candidate below 60% be admitted to the examination**, even with medical documentation.
- The Director's condonation decision is final at the institutional level.

5.4.2 The condonation application must be submitted to the HOD at least **7 days before** the commencement of the End-Semester Examination. Applications submitted after this deadline will not be processed.

5.4.3 Condonation is granted on a per-subject basis. A student may receive condonation for some subjects and remain barred in others.

### 5.5 Special Academic Council Dispensation

**Clause 4.12.3**: The Academic Council reserves the extraordinary authority to condone student absence of up to **30%** on sports and/or medical grounds, thereby permitting students with an overall attendance of **not less than 50%** to write the regular semester examinations. This power is exercised only in extraordinary circumstances and is not a routine provision.

> **[CONTRADICTION POINT A — see contradictions.md]**: §5.4 (Clause 4.10.4) asserts that the Director can condone to a floor of 60% and that "under no circumstances" shall any candidate below 60% be admitted. §5.5 (Clause 4.12.3) grants the Academic Council authority to permit students with as little as 50% to appear. These two clauses are irreconcilable on their face: the Director's absolute bar at 60% is overridden by the Academic Council's 50% floor — but the mechanism for initiating Academic Council dispensation, and the conditions under which it supersedes the Director's decision, are never stated. A student with 55% attendance cannot determine from the document alone whether they can appear for the examination.

5.5.2 Requests for Academic Council dispensation must be submitted in writing to the Dean (Academics) office with full supporting documentation. The Academic Council meets on a scheduled basis; emergency sessions may be convened at the Director's request.

### 5.6 Attendance for NCC/NSS and Other Official Activities

5.6.1 Students participating in officially sanctioned activities shall be granted attendance for the missed sessions, provided prior approval is obtained from the Dean (Student Welfare) and the subject teacher is notified in advance. The following activities qualify:

| Activity | Maximum Days Attendance Credited |
|---|---|
| NCC Annual Training Camp | Up to 14 days |
| NSS Special Camp | Up to 7 days |
| Representation in National/State Sports | Up to 21 days |
| Institute-Approved Technical Events (Inter-collegiate) | Up to 3 days |
| Participation in International Competitions | Up to 30 days |

5.6.2 Attendance for official activities shall be counted as "Present" for the purposes of attendance percentage calculation. However, CE marks for missed sessions (tests, assignments, etc.) must be compensated through alternative assessment methods as decided by the subject teacher.

## Chapter 6 — Examinations

### 6.1 Examination Schedule

6.1.1 The End-Semester Examination schedule shall be notified by the Controller of Examinations at least **30 days** before the commencement of examinations.

6.1.2 A student may apply for rescheduling of an examination clash (two exams at the same time) within **5 days** of the publication of the schedule.

### 6.2 Hall Ticket

6.2.1 Hall Tickets for End-Semester Examinations are issued by the Examination Section. Students must carry their Hall Ticket and their **physical Institute Identity Card** to every examination session. Entry to the examination hall without these documents will not be permitted.

6.2.2 Loss of Hall Ticket must be reported to the Examination Section at least **24 hours** before the relevant examination. Duplicate Hall Tickets may be issued against a prescribed fee of ₹100.

### 6.3 Examination Conduct

6.3.1 Students must be seated in the examination hall **15 minutes before** the commencement of the examination. Students arriving after the examination has begun may be allowed entry at the invigilator's discretion but shall not be given any additional time.

6.3.2 No student shall be permitted to leave the examination hall within the **first 60 minutes** of the examination.

6.3.3 Mobile phones and any electronic communication devices are **strictly prohibited** inside the examination hall. Possession of such a device, whether switched off or not, shall constitute use of Unfair Means and shall be dealt with under Chapter 9.

6.3.4 Students who need to use the restroom during an examination shall be accompanied by an invigilator or an authorised attendant.

### 6.4 Backlog / Supplementary Examinations

6.4.1 Students who have failed in one or more subjects (Grade F or Ab) are eligible to appear in the supplementary (backlog) examination conducted at the end of the subsequent semester.

6.4.2 A student may carry up to **4 backlogs** without being detained. A student with more than 4 backlogs at the end of any academic year is detained under Clause 4.7.1(b).

6.4.3 A maximum of **three attempts** (the regular attempt plus two subsequent attempts) are permitted for clearing a backlog subject. After three failed attempts, the student's case is referred to the Academic Council for a special decision, which may include:
- Granting a fourth attempt under strict conditions;
- Recommending withdrawal from the programme;
- Permitting the student to continue without the credit (only where the subject is elective and an alternative exists).

6.4.4 Backlog examination marks are recorded on the transcript. The final mark used for CGPA calculation upon passing a backlog subject is the mark secured in the passing attempt (not capped, but the transcript records all attempts).

### 6.5 Postponement of Examinations

6.5.1 In the event of unforeseen circumstances (natural disaster, citywide emergency, epidemic, law and order issues), the Controller of Examinations is empowered to postpone any scheduled examination. The revised schedule shall be notified on the Institute's official portal and notice boards.

## Chapter 7 — Academic Probation and Warning

### 7.1 Academic Warning

7.1.1 A student whose CGPA falls below **5.0** at the end of any semester will be placed on **Academic Warning**. The Academic Warning is recorded in the student's academic file and the student is required to meet their Faculty Advisor and HOD within one week.

7.1.2 Continuing on Academic Warning for more than **two consecutive semesters** shall result in the student being referred to the Academic Council for review.

### 7.2 Academic Probation

7.2.1 A student whose CGPA falls below **4.5** after two consecutive semesters of Academic Warning shall be placed on **Academic Probation**.

7.2.2 A student on Academic Probation must submit an Academic Improvement Plan (AIP) to their HOD within 15 days. The AIP shall specify the student's strategy to improve their CGPA, with specific targets.

## Chapter 8 — Examination of the Major Project (Semester VIII)

### 8.1 Eligibility for Major Project

8.1.1 A student is eligible to register for the Major Project (Semester VIII) if:
(a) The student has cleared all subjects of Semesters I through VI with no active backlogs; OR
(b) The student has at most **2 active backlogs** from Semester VII, with a CGPA of **6.0 or above**.

8.1.2 Major Project must be undertaken in a recognized organization, which includes:
(a) AICTE or UGC-approved academic institutions;
(b) CSIR, DST, DRDO, or other government R&D laboratories;
(c) Listed public limited companies or MSME-registered enterprises with a valid Udyam certificate;
(d) Government ministries, public sector undertakings, or statutory bodies.

8.1.3 Project undertaken in an unregistered or unrecognized organization will not be accepted for credit.

### 8.2 Evaluation of Major Project

8.2.1 Major Project evaluation shall have two components:
- Internal Assessment by the departmental guide and a panel of internal examiners: 60%
- External Viva Voce before an expert nominated by the Controller of Examinations: 40%

## Chapter 9 — Unfair Means (UFM)

### 9.1 Acts Constituting UFM

9.1.1 The following acts, or any attempt thereof, shall constitute Unfair Means:
(a) Writing or copying from another student's answer script;
(b) Possessing written material or electronic devices in the examination hall;
(c) Receiving or transmitting information through any means during an examination;
(d) Impersonating another student or allowing oneself to be impersonated;
(e) Misbehaviour with invigilators or examination staff;
(f) Submitting fabricated attendance records, medical certificates, or other documents.

### 9.2 UFM Penalties

9.2.1 UFM cases are referred to the UFM Committee constituted by the Director. Penalties include:

| Severity | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Minor (possession of material, not used) | Cancellation of the paper; awarded zero in that paper |
| Moderate (copying detected, partial) | Cancellation of the entire semester's examination |
| Major (impersonation, organized cheating) | Cancellation of all examinations of that year + 1-year suspension |
| Repeat offence | Expulsion from the Institute |

9.2.2 Students found guilty of UFM shall also have a permanent notation on their academic transcript.

9.2.3 A student subjected to UFM proceedings is barred from participating in any placement activities, campus interviews, or co-curricular competitions until the matter is resolved.

## Chapter 10 — Leave and Absence

### 10.1 Application for Leave

10.1.1 Students must apply for leave in advance through the online portal or by submitting a written application to the Head of Department. Leave applications submitted after the absence shall not be considered except in genuine emergency situations with documentary evidence.

### 10.2 Medical Leave

10.2.1 Medical leave may be granted upon submission of a medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner. For absences of more than 3 consecutive days, a certificate from a government hospital or a recognized private hospital is required.

10.2.2 Medical leave does not automatically convert attendance from "Absent" to "Present". Medical leave is relevant only for:
(a) Condonation applications under Clause 4.10.4 (§5.4);
(b) Exemption from CE components missed during the medical period, subject to the faculty's discretion.

### 10.3 Maternity Leave

10.3.1 Female students are entitled to **60 days of maternity leave** on submission of a doctor's certificate confirming pregnancy. Maternity leave shall be granted as **Authorised Absence** and shall be treated as "attended" for the purpose of attendance percentage calculation, subject to a maximum of one instance per academic year.

## Chapter 11 — Degree Award

### 11.1 Requirements for Award of Degree

11.1.1 The B.Tech degree shall be awarded upon:
(a) Successful completion of all required credits (minimum 170);
(b) A minimum CGPA of **5.0** at the end of the programme;
(c) Clearance of all dues to the Institute (tuition fees, library fines, laboratory breakage charges, hostel dues, etc.) — commonly referred to as "No Dues";
(d) No pending disciplinary proceedings.

11.1.2 A student who completes all credits but has a CGPA below 5.0 shall be awarded a **B.Tech (Pass)** degree at the discretion of the Academic Council.

### 11.2 Convocation

11.2.1 Degrees are formally conferred at the Annual Convocation. Students who have satisfied the degree requirements but are unable to attend the Convocation may collect their degree certificate from the Academic Section upon submission of a written request.

### 11.3 Provisional Certificate

11.3.1 Upon completion of requirements, a Provisional Certificate is issued pending the formal Convocation. The Provisional Certificate is valid for all employment and higher-education purposes.
